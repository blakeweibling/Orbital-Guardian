ORBITAL MECHANICS & SGP4 TELEMETRY ARCHITECTURE

1. Keplerian & SGP4 Orbital Propagation

Each tracked resident space object (RSO) is propagated in real time using six classical Keplerian orbital elements derived from Two-Line Element (TLE) sets: Semi-major axis (a), Eccentricity (e), Inclination (i), Right Ascension of the Ascending Node (Ω), Argument of Perigee (ω), and Mean Anomaly (M₀). Kepler's equation M = E - e·sin(E) is solved iteratively via Newton-Raphson at every frame, incorporating secular J2 nodal precession (dΩ/dt) and apsidal rotation (dω/dt) caused by Earth's oblateness.

2. High-Density Space Debris Shells

  • 780–860 km Sun-Synchronous LEO: Highest debris concentration in Earth orbit, dominated by the 2007 Fengyun-1C ASAT test (>3,500 fragments) and the 2009 Iridium 33 / Cosmos 2251 hypervelocity collision (>2,300 fragments).
  • 480–560 km Commercial LEO: Occupied by the ISS (~418 km), Cosmos 1408 ASAT fragments (~480 km), and dense commercial broadband constellations (~550 km).
  • 20,200 km Semi-Synchronous MEO: Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GPS, Galileo, GLONASS, BeiDou) with 12-hour orbital periods.
  • 35,786 km Geostationary & Graveyard Belt: Communications satellites matching Earth's sidereal rotation period (23h 56m 4s), flanked by +300 km super-synchronous disposal orbits.
